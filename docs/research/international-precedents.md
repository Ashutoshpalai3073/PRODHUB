# International Precedents — and three corrections to the brief

Evidence for Sanyog's **scale-up pathway (Stage 8)**, **milestone payments (Stage 6)**,
**IP clauses (Stage 5)** and **independent validation (Stage 7)**.

## ⚠️ THREE CORRECTIONS — read before writing the deck

### Correction 1 — EU PCP **forbids** the thing we want to cite it for
The official EC PCP tender template states ✅ PRIMARY:

> "PCP contractors **may not be given any preferential treatment in a subsequent procurement**
> for provision of the final products or services on a commercial scale."

**So never cite EU PCP as precedent for "scale up without re-tendering."** It is the precedent
for *phased de-risking and IP sharing* only. For non-competed scale-up, cite the **US**
authorities below, or the EU's **Innovation Partnership** (Art. 31, Dir. 2014/24/EU), where the
commercial phase is lawful precisely because it was competed at the outset.
*This is the single most likely thing a hostile judge catches.*

### Correction 2 — "30% on onboarding" is not a valid payment trigger
The problem statement proposes **30% on onboarding/setup**. Under **FAR 32.1004** ✅ PRIMARY,
milestone events must be *"integral and necessary"* to performance and the buyer must be able to
*"readily verify successful performance"* — and the rule **expressly prohibits contract
signing, option exercise or "passage of time" as payment triggers.** Payment on onboarding is
payment on signature.

**The fix is framing, not arithmetic.** Call tranche 1 a **mobilisation advance** — a distinct
instrument secured by a bank guarantee — and keep tranches 2 and 3 as genuine verifiable
milestones:

> **30% secured mobilisation advance → 40% on mid-term KPI demonstration → 30% on independent
> validation.**

That reads as fully defensible and matches real practice (Singapore OIP runs exactly **30% / 70%**
✅; CivTech pays £40k as **5 × £8k** with the last tranche released only after Demo Day ✅).
🟡 GFR 2017 **Rule 172** reportedly caps advances to a private firm at **30%** — if that holds,
our first tranche sits exactly at the Indian ceiling, which is a strong defensibility point.
**Verify against the official GFR before printing it.**

### Correction 3 — escrow: it's *deliverable* escrow, not *payment* escrow
The problem statement says "escrow-backed milestone payouts." **Payment escrow is not standard
practice in public innovation procurement anywhere** I could verify. What *is* standard is
**source-code / deliverable escrow** — the EC PCP template ✅ instructs buyers to specify

> "whether the contractor is required to deposit copies of results (e.g. the source code and
> design specifications) … under an **ESCROW agreement** designed to guarantee the buyers group
> continued access to results in the case of financial bankruptcy of the contractor."

Position Sanyog's escrow as (a) **deliverable escrow** protecting the department if the startup
fails, and/or (b) a **payment-assurance ledger** making the committed tranche visible and
auditable. Do not claim escrowed public payments are international practice.

## 1. ⭐ The legal basis for scale-up without re-tendering

**15 U.S.C. §638(r)(4)** ✅ PRIMARY, verbatim:
> "Federal agencies and Federal prime contractors shall … **(B) issue, without further
> justification, Phase III awards** relating to technology, **including sole source awards**, to
> the SBIR and STTR award recipients that developed the technology."

**SBA SBIR/STTR Policy Directive §4(c)(3)** ✅ PRIMARY:
> "The competitions for SBIR/STTR Phase I and Phase II awards satisfy any competition
> requirement… An agency that wishes to fund an SBIR/STTR Phase III award … **is not required to
> conduct another competition** … **Further justification is not needed.**"

§4(c)(5): *"There is **no limit** on the number, duration, type, or dollar value of Phase III
awards."*

**Even cleaner — DoD Other Transaction Authority, 10 U.S.C. §4022(f)** ✅ PRIMARY:
> "A follow-on production contract … **may be awarded to the participants … without the use of
> competitive procedures** … if— (A) **competitive procedures were used** for the selection of
> parties …; and (B) the participants **successfully completed the prototype project**."

> **This is exactly Sanyog's design in one sentence:** *competitive entry + independently
> verified successful pilot = lawful non-competed scale-up.* Cite §4022(f) alongside §638(r)(4).

⚠️ **Do NOT claim FAR 6.302-5 names SBIR Phase III — it does not.** FAR Part 6 was read
directly; there is no mention of SBIR or 15 U.S.C. 638. Say instead: *"a J&A under FAR 6.302-5
citing 15 U.S.C. §638(r)(4)."*

## 2. UK SBRI — the IP clause to copy

Now "Innovate UK Contracts for Innovation". R&D bought as a **contract, not a grant**:
*"Developments are 100% funded"*; *"at least 50% of the contract value attributed directly and
exclusively to R&D services"* ✅.

**The actual published IP clause** (BEIS Pre-Commercial T&Cs, Condition 27) ✅ PRIMARY:
> "(2) any **Arising Intellectual Property shall belong to the Contractor**.
> (3) The Contractor hereby grants to the Authority a **worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free,
> non-exclusive licence at no cost … together with the right to grant sublicences**, to use or
> publish any Arising Intellectual Property … **for its non-commercial purposes**."

Plus a **march-in**: if the IP is not commercially exploited within **5 years**, the Authority
can require assignment or third-party licensing; ownership cannot be transferred for **10 years**
without consent (Condition 28).

> **This is the model for Sanyog's Stage 5** — and note the precision that matters: the licence
> is for the buyer's **non-commercial purposes**, not a blanket licence. That is what makes
> "startup retains IP" real rather than cosmetic.

**Definitions worth lifting verbatim:** *Background IP* = owned before the contract; *Arising IP*
= created as a result of performing the services. (Horizon Europe calls these **Background /
Results**; "foreground" is the older FP7 term.)

**Scale and outcomes** ✅ (UKRI/Steer evaluation 2022, 2008–2020): **£788m** across **578
competitions**; Phase 1 avg **£97k** (3,562 awards), Phase 2 avg **£493k** (852 awards); **26%**
of applicants awarded; **benefit-cost ratio 1.53–4.07**; **+9.4% headcount** for SBRI firms.
⚠️ No statistically significant effect on turnover or productivity — quote the BCR and
employment figure, not a turnover claim.

**Typical phase shape:** £50k / 3 months / ~30 projects → £1m / 9 months / **10 contracts**.
A 30→10 funnel with a **20× value step**.

## 3. 🎯 The strongest argument in the entire evidence base

The UK's own evaluation of SBRI admits ✅ PRIMARY:

> "**UKRI do not collate or capture any public sector impacts** within their central monitoring
> database and the majority of departments/agencies consulted with **have not been able to
> provide data on quantified public sector impacts**."

And on the UK GovTech Catalyst specifically:

> "GOVTECH and Seafood Innovation Fund together equate to programme spending worth **£30m, but
> to date no products have been procured as a result of the competitions**."

The US has the same blind spot: SBA told Congressional Research Service it cannot require
companies to report commercialisation, so Phase II→III conversion data "has limited use."

> **Pitch line:** *The world's most-copied startup-procurement mechanisms cannot tell you whether
> their pilots were ever actually bought. Sanyog instruments the pilot→procurement conversion as
> a first-class object — which is the number every one of these programmes is missing.*

That reframes Sanyog from "another challenge portal" into the thing that fixes the measured
failure of every existing programme. **This should be a slide.**

## 4. ⭐ The KPI schema to build — NHS England, live 2026

NHS England's Cancer Programme Innovation Open Call makes independent evaluation an **eligibility
condition** ✅ PRIMARY:

> "Every innovation is **required to complete an independent evaluation**."
> "Applications are open to … organisations that have a demonstratable partnership with an NHS
> organisation **and an independent evaluation partner**."
> "The **strength of your evaluation design will be a selection criterion**."

**Their published KPI table headers are, effectively, the data model for a pilot KPI** ✅:

| Value domain | Sub-question | KPI description | **Baseline value** | **Target value (+ timepoint)** | **Is this a go/no-go indicator? (Y/N)** | **Go/no-go threshold to trigger commissioning decision** | **Measurement frequency** | **Data source** | **Responsible organisation** |

> If Sanyog builds one thing from this brief, build that table. It already encodes baseline,
> target, timepoint, a boolean scale-gate flag, an explicit commissioning threshold, cadence,
> provenance and accountable party.

**Their funding taper is the scale-up gate to copy** ✅:
> "Year 1: NHS England funds **100%**. Year 2: costs **shared**. Year 3: **100% local** funding…
> The achievement of a defined set of outcomes **by an agreed Go/No Go decision point in Year 2
> will unlock full funding for delivery in Year 3**."

**Methodology:** a **counterfactual** is required — *"what would have happened in the absence of
the policy"* — via experimental (RCT), quasi-experimental (matched comparison), or
non-experimental (pre/post) design, with **contribution analysis** as the named fallback.

## 5. Other reusable structures

**CivTech (Scotland)** ✅ — closest working analogue to Sanyog:
3 teams × **£5,000 / 3 weeks** (Exploration) → **£40,000 / 15 weeks** paid as **5 × £8,000**,
last tranche after Demo Day (Accelerator) → **up to £595,000** Pre-Commercial Agreement
negotiated directly, **not re-tendered**. IP stays with the company; the sponsor gets a
**perpetual royalty-free licence**; *"neither CivTech nor the Challenge Sponsors take any equity."*

**Singapore IMDA Open Innovation Platform** ✅ — the 30% precedent: finalists get *"a stipulated
proportion, **typically 30%**, of the prize money"* up front, **70%** on completion. Staged
government contracting: PoC (Stage 1) → pilot trial (Stage 2) → deployment (Stage 3), with the
agency deciding whether to proceed. IP: *"all IP created … is to be owned by the Solution
Provider."*

**EU PCP phase rules** ✅ — minimum **3 phases**, starting with **≥3 contractors**, ending with
**≥2**. Published outcomes: **half** of solutions deployed within a year; **73.5%** of contracts
to SMEs/startups; prices **50% lower** with **4× more** offers; commercialisation **86% / 75% /
29%** by phase reached.

**Liability caps — the UK convention** ✅: SBRI uses *"**£4,000,000 or twice the contract value,
whichever is the greater**"*; CCS Short Form **125%** of charges; Model Services Contract **150%**
(200% for wilful breach); DASA **£5m**. Always **uncapped**: death/personal injury, fraud,
bribery, title warranties, and the **IP indemnity**. Data protection is handled three different
ways — pick one deliberately and say why.

**Payment timelines** ✅: India **45 days** (MSMED s.15, 15 days absent a written agreement);
US **30 days** with a **15-day accelerated goal for small business** (FAR 32.009-1); EU
**30/60 days** at ECB+8pp plus €40.
> **Clean, citable gap:** India's 45-day limit is stricter than the EU's 60-day ceiling — but
> India has **no equivalent of the US 15-day accelerated small-business goal.** Sanyog can claim
> to close exactly that gap.
